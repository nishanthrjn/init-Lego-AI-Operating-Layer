## 1. Context and decision question

ADR-004 establishes atomic owner-local state/result/obligation commits. ADR-007 orders protected handoff against current invalidating mutations, preserves partial claim consumption and records a durable handoff-attempt obligation before actual acceptance. Neither makes a remote effect atomic with a local commit. ADR-009 separates producing facts from mandatory Evidence custody and requires repair without replaying completed work.

How should the reference runtime recognize equivalent submissions, recover permitted prior outcomes, bound deduplication coverage and reconcile uncertain effects across linked attempts?

The first scope remains the trusted local reference runtime, one explicit tenant and the exclusive dispatch domain accepted in ADR-007. Direct Capability execution must work without Agent, Model, Workflow or Team dependencies. External systems remain independent authorities for their own effects. Distributed failover, hostile shared-process isolation and universal exactly-once delivery are outside this decision's guarantees.

Priority is prevention of additional unintended effects, truthful uncertainty and feasible retention, followed by simple local operation and replaceability. Availability may decrease when evidence or authority is missing. Existing canonical states, error categories, owner boundaries and explicit current authorization remain unchanged.

## 2. Alternatives and evidence

### A — Owner-local operation ledgers with retained outcomes

Each owner atomically stores the operation's equivalence evidence and result alongside its state mutation and obligations. The invoking plane owns the intended-effect record and dispatch obligation; other owners expose their own status. This aligns directly with ADR-004 and avoids an additional authoritative global journal. It requires explicit references and reconciliation across partially completed owners.

### B — Coordinated operation journal with owner references

A common journal can simplify navigation, scheduling and inspection of cross-owner work. It cannot by itself prove an owner's commit or external effect. Making it authoritative introduces another commit gap or requires broad transactions; leaving it observational still requires A's owner records. This is useful later as a derived inspection view, but is not selected as a new authority or prerequisite.

### C — External reconciliation Adapters with bounded provider deduplication

Provider keys, status queries and attributed observations may close uncertainty for particular effects. Their scope, retention, target identity and failure behavior must be demonstrated. They do not cover local Approval/resource commits, different providers or every business effect. This is selected only as a qualified extension to A for supported external effects, not as its replacement.

### D — Defer effects and retries

Valid for inert structural work or an explicitly non-effectful early scope. It cannot satisfy the selected usable effectful reference slice, so it is not the resolution of this P1 decision.

| Criterion | A — Owner ledgers | B — Common journal | C — Provider mechanisms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner commit deduplication | Atomic with owner's result | Still needs owner guard | Does not supply it |
| Cross-owner partial facts | Explicit referenced outcomes | Easier inspection; no automatic atomicity | Remain local responsibilities |
| External lost acknowledgment | Preserves uncertainty | Preserves uncertainty | May resolve within proven scope |
| Retention | Per owner and obligation | Journal and owners must stay aligned | Provider horizon adds a bound |
| Local development | Fits accepted storage boundary | Additional coordination component | Provider simulator/qualification needed |
| Failure isolation | Owner unavailable is visible | Additional central availability risk | Independent outages/expiry |
| Replacement | Preserve ledger semantics | Journal migration plus owners | Equivalence may not survive change |

Evidence here is analytical comparison against accepted contracts and the failure schedules in §12. No provider documentation, experiments, benchmark or implementation results establish a stronger guarantee. A provider-specific support claim must bring that evidence before admission.

## 3. Decision

**Select A, augmented by C only where a qualified Adapter proves the required end-to-end behavior.**

1. Every supported mutable owner retains a scoped operation ledger within its ADR-004 atomic commit boundary.
2. The invoking plane retains a separate intended-effect record, linked dispatch knowledge, participant outcomes and outstanding obligations. Action Mediation owns this for Tools.
3. Equivalence is determined from a versioned, contract-defined semantic descriptor, including original preconditions. Identity is not inferred from a payload hash or similar text.
4. Retain a small complete result envelope locally; pin exact governed references for large result payloads where their availability/access contract supports the declared result guarantee.
5. Declare finite profile-specific retry coverage before enabling a retry claim. Couple that coverage to local retention, evidence duties and any external deduplication coverage.
6. Unknown handoff/effect is reconciled or held for entitled intervention. Recovery does not automatically repeat an unresolved dispatch.

These are behavioral records under existing owners, not new canonical entities, wire schemas or a global Execution state machine. The Kernel acquires no operation journal, authority or resource ledger. A shared library may implement mechanics under ADR-002 without becoming an owner or importing another owner's implementation state.

## 4. Identity and equivalence

### 4.1 Identity domains

| Identity | Meaning and lifetime |
|---|---|
| Operation identity | One logical owner operation within owner/contract and verified tenant/subject scope; stable for equivalent retransmissions |
| Request identity | One submission; retransmitting that submission retains it; a new submission has its own identity |
| Execution identity | One execution attempt; a genuine execution retry creates a linked new Execution |
| Effect identity | One intended protected effect in tenant/subject scope, preserved across compatible attempts and provider recovery |
| Transport attempt identity | One delivery attempt; cannot replace effect identity or prove effect uniqueness |
| Observation/correction identity | One attributed observation or correction; repeats cannot apply it twice |

Use opaque identifiers with authoritative scoped expected-absence/uniqueness guards. Allocation collisions cause a conflict, never overwrite. No UUID variant, hash algorithm, wire format or datastore is chosen. Identifier secrecy is not an authorization mechanism. Incoming identities must be scope-checked; they cannot reveal another tenant's existence.

Changing request or Execution identity cannot allocate a second dispatch slot for the same intended effect. Conversely, content similarity cannot collapse two deliberately distinct intended effects into one. A new intended effect requires explicit new intent and independent authority, with links to relevant prior work and uncertainty.

### 4.2 Semantic descriptor and comparison

The owner stores the original operation meaning: contract/version and operation name; verified tenant/subject and originating requester; exact typed inputs/action/target meaning; governing immutable revisions; original expected revision or expected-absence condition; applicable intended-effect identity and equivalence rule/version. Exact immutable references include the interpretation needed to understand them after upgrades.

Comparison follows the declared contract's normalization rules. Field ordering or another explicitly equivalent representation may compare equal; omitted versus null, units, numeric conversions, list ordering and defaults may differ and must not be guessed. Undeclared coercion, approximate text similarity and model judgment are not equivalence mechanisms. Unsupported interpretation returns VersionMismatch or the applicable canonical validation failure without dispatch.

A digest may accelerate lookup or verify integrity, but is not sole semantic proof. Preserve sufficient exact descriptor evidence and its interpretation; detect conflicting meaning even if a lookup digest collides. Sensitive inputs may use immutable protected references only if their exact meaning remains verifiable within the guarantee. Do not retain raw Secrets merely to reproduce an old request.

Current authorization evidence may refresh eligibility without changing the original requester, intended action, immutable revisions or expected preconditions. Credential rotation cannot silently substitute a target or widen purpose. A materially changed operation gets new intent/identity and fresh governed checks; it does not rewrite the old ledger entry.

## 5. Owner-local commit and duplicate protocol

For a mutable operation:

1. Verify the request's current identity and tenant scope; validate its interpretable semantic descriptor without protected existence disclosure.
2. Inspect retained operation evidence through the owner. An equivalent committed operation returns its currently permitted prior outcome. Do this before treating the original expected revision as a new stale-write conflict.
3. Conflicting reuse of the same scoped identity returns Conflict, with only authorized diagnostics. Missing/unavailable evidence is handled under §8; it is not automatically fresh intent.
4. For proven new admissible work, check current mutation authority, policy, expected revision/absence and owner invariants.
5. At the commit boundary, atomically enforce uniqueness/equivalence again and commit state, immutable history, result envelope, interpretation evidence and required obligations. A preliminary lookup is not the race guard.
6. Return the durable outcome. Lost acknowledgment leads to status/retransmission of the same operation, not a compensating second mutation.

Two concurrent equivalent submissions can observe no initial result, but only the guarded owner commit establishes it; the other observes that result or a truthful pending/unavailable outcome. A genuinely separate operation with the same expected revision may conflict. The original committed equivalent operation does not conflict solely because its own resulting revision advanced the head.

Current disclosure authority is independent of historical authorization to mutate. A caller who can still inspect but can no longer mutate may receive a permitted historical result; a revoked caller cannot obtain it through deduplication. Neither refusal erases the commit or makes repeating it safe.

## 6. Outcome retention and status queries

The durable result envelope retains original equivalence/preconditions, recorded acknowledgment stage, produced owner revisions, exact output references or small permitted values, errors and recoverability, named effects and their statuses, participant references, obligations and coverage metadata. It describes the committed outcome, not the current head substituted for that outcome.

Full large-payload retention in every ledger is rejected: it duplicates Artifacts/evidence, expands privacy exposure and increases cost. Exact governed references are selected when the referenced owner can supply immutable content, historical interpretation, integrity and access for the promised interval. A mutable “latest” URL is insufficient. If a contract promises a complete retrievable result, pin/retain the payload accordingly or do not admit that guarantee.

When referenced data is unavailable, status can still truthfully report an authorized known commit/effect and missing output. It must not fabricate output, claim a full successful retrieval, or rerun the effect to regenerate data. Current retention/privacy disposition may restrict disclosure; a reference never bypasses it. Claims must distinguish retention of deduplication evidence from availability of full result content.

Every owner exposes `get_operation_status` or the explicitly equivalent family query required by CTR-015. It reports authorized recorded stages, exact results/revisions, freshness, effects, obligations and retained coverage. “No retained record” is separate from authoritative absence. Querying one participant does not establish every other participant's state. Queries remain resource-bounded and may generate governed access evidence.

## 7. Dispatch, provider deduplication and external effects

### 7.1 Single invoking-plane responsibility

Preserve ADR-007's gate, current full claim set, durable attempt obligation and actual acceptance boundary. An operation ledger is not a second dispatcher. Claim consumption, durable intent, handoff acceptance and confirmed external effect remain distinct observations. Recovery of an unresolved attempt does not release it again automatically, even if its last local fact precedes actual acceptance.

Confirmed owner-local state mutation can be deduplicated under ADR-004; a local intended-effect record alone cannot deduplicate a remote business effect. Any retry that can reach a protected boundary must meet current ADR-005/007 authority, Approval, deadline, evidence and resource requirements. Historical permission is not reusable permission.

### 7.2 Qualification for an external equivalent retransmission

Before admitting it, the Adapter's declared profile must demonstrate all of:

- Stable mapping of the same intended effect and exact action to the provider's scoped key, target/account and operation semantics; conflicting key reuse is rejected.
- Atomic provider handling of concurrent duplicates and truthful prior-result/status behavior for the named effect, rather than only HTTP receipt suppression.
- A declared validity interval, its start/reset rules, time uncertainty and retention guarantees covering the proposed delivery. Local retention cannot extend a provider's expired coverage.
- Failure coverage including lost acknowledgment, timeout, reconnect and provider restart where claimed; hidden SDK, proxy and provider retries are disabled or included in the proof.
- Current authorized transport/Secret use, disclosure and resource bounds; new credential material preserves action equivalence and does not reset identity.
- A bounded local acceptance mechanism consistent with ADR-007. Remote response waiting does not move into the final gate.

Qualification permits a separately governed continuation under the original effect record and exclusive invoking-plane responsibility, not a second independent dispatch obligation or blanket replay of unknown attempts. The continuation has its own recorded transport attempt, current admission and auditable proof that the provider's equivalent handling prevents another intended effect. If this cannot be demonstrated, query/reconcile or hold.

No external retransmission profile is prequalified by this ADR. A non-deduplicating provider may support a single admitted handoff with an explicitly uncertain outcome and intervention path, if the capability accepts that guarantee; it cannot advertise safe automatic resend.

### 7.3 Provider replacement

Changing Provider does not change effect identity or erase the old target's history. Keys local to two providers do not establish a common deduplication domain. Replacement requires proof that the same effect/equivalence and already-performed status are preserved, or reconciliation followed by an explicitly authorized safe strategy. Unknown effect at A cannot be treated as none at B. A truly different intended action is new governed work with its duplicate-risk relationship disclosed.

## 8. Coverage, retention and safe absence

Each supported operation/profile declares identity scope, equivalence version, retry-valid interval and time basis, covered failure domain, status behavior, result accessibility and required owner/evidence retention. The accountable contract/profile and retention owners approve actual durations before enablement; this ADR invents no universal TTL or unlimited storage promise.

For any proposed equivalent external retry, its permitted interval is bounded by every applicable coverage interval: local operation/effect evidence, provider deduplication and other required participants. Intersect actual intervals, accounting for uncertainty and different start times; taking the smallest nominal duration is insufficient. Current deadlines/authority may shorten eligibility further. A later profile revision cannot retroactively shorten an already promised retention guarantee without explicit governed treatment of affected operations.

Retain enough equivalence, outcome and lineage evidence for at least the advertised coverage and any longer unresolved-obligation or mandatory evidence duty. Expiry stops a retry guarantee; it does not extinguish unknown effect, consumed Approval, charge liability or outstanding repair. Bounded admission accounts for that storage obligation before creating work. If obligations outlive estimates, apply authorized retention/capacity handling and restrict new admission rather than silently deleting safety evidence.

Garbage collection is governed owner work. After full result expiry, a minimal retained identity/coverage marker may prevent mistaken reuse, subject to disposition policy; it does not falsely claim payload availability. If even that marker must be removed, expired identities must still not enter a path that interprets missing records as proof of new work. An admission profile must prove request freshness and authoritative absence for the relevant scope, or require reconciliation/intervention. Generating a new identifier is not a safe substitute for that proof.

Authoritative absence requires complete relevant owner history/coverage and a proven absence of prior commit or possible handoff, including in-flight dispatch obligations. For external work it also requires authoritative evidence that an earlier request cannot still produce the effect. An eventually consistent provider “not found,” a missing local row, restored old snapshot or empty cache is insufficient. If the profile cannot supply such evidence, absence remains unestablished.

Mandatory deletion, unavailable historical interpretation or lost records may make a former guarantee impossible. Preserve the fact and scope of the gap where permitted, disclose unavailability and block dependent retries. Resolve disposition under ADR-009 and accountable authority; do not invent retention-policy precedence or regenerate missing evidence by repeating its producing effect.

## 9. Reconciliation, obligations and compensation

Reconciliation is a distinct governed operation with its own identity, expected observation/obligation revision, current Principal/Tenant authority, bounded resources and due conditions. It gathers attributable evidence from relevant owners/provider targets, records a linked observation and updates only the responsible owner's state. Duplicate observation/correction submissions cannot apply twice.

Preserve canonical effect statuses **none, partial, completed, unknown** per named effect. Timeout and missing audit evidence do not prove none. A completed investigation may leave the effect unknown; it cannot satisfy an obligation requiring confirmation. Contradictory observations remain linked and visible under disclosure policy until authorized resolution. A later correction never overwrites the original assertion/history.

Every cross-owner operation exposes CTR-056 obligation descriptors: identity, owner, responsible requester/Principal, tenant, parent operation/effect, required action/target revision, due/retention bound, guarantee, pending/satisfied/failed/requires-intervention status, last evidence/error, next safe action and reconciliation authority. These are not new Execution states. Parent completion depends on its declared postconditions, not merely the reconciler finishing or every participant returning an acknowledgment.

Repair the actual outstanding duty: Evidence custody after an effect, Catalog publication after initialization, accounting after usage, or old-release stop after migration. Never repeat the producing action to obtain missing evidence. ADR-009 source facts and custody obligations remain independent; effect success with custody failure remains partial parent completion.

Compensation is new governed work with a new intended effect, independent authority/Approval/resource admission and its own failure/uncertainty record. It links to the original effect without erasing it or guaranteeing rollback. An authorized exceptional reissue likewise records explicit new intent and remaining duplicate risk; intervention cannot manufacture proof that the prior effect was absent.

## 10. Retransmission, attempts and resource continuity

Equivalent retransmission retains original operation identity, meaning and preconditions. A genuine execution retry creates a new linked Execution while retaining the intended-effect identity whenever it relies on equivalence. Neither resets inherited deadline, attempt limits, cumulative consumption, unknown liability or child allocation accounting. Nested retries must carry the same lineage; a child cannot evade a parent's exhausted bounds.

Use the contract's Q/M/X/F retry meanings. Category or a “potentially retryable” label does not authorize a new effect. Retry policy must specify eligible errors, remaining bounds, current authority and treatment of earlier effects. DeadlineExceeded can coexist with completed or unknown effect. AuthorizationDenied and TenantViolation cannot be bypassed by changing identifiers. Conflict does not permit silently substituting the latest expected revision.

Approval consumed before failed dispatch remains consumed. A new entitlement requires its own governed decision; no recovery worker refunds it automatically. Reservation expiry, cancellation, retry abandonment and provider change do not release incurred or unknown liability. Unique usage/settlement operations use ADR-004/007 owner guards so duplicate reports do not double-charge or double-release. Status queries, reconciliation and transport retries can themselves cost resources.

## 11. Failure model and recovery

The local guarantee inherits ADR-004's qualified durable owner storage and ADR-007's exclusive process/domain with fail-stop recovery. Concurrent tasks and process or admitted machine restart with intact qualified storage are in scope. Storage loss/rollback, conflicting surviving dispatchers, independent remote state changes and partitions receive only the guarantees explicitly demonstrated by their profiles. An epoch label or Python lock is not fencing proof.

On recovery: establish exclusive authority; recover owner-local results and obligations; inspect the original operation/effect lineage; distinguish known commit from unknown handoff; obtain current access and admission for each next action. Do not replay a queue of unresolved effects as an initialization convenience. A stale process must be unable to write or reach acceptance before replacement starts.

Guarantee statements must name the boundary: one guarded owner commit within retained coverage, one independent dispatch obligation, provider-deduplicated equivalent attempts within its interval, or one confirmed external effect. None implies the others. No universal exactly-once claim, delivery-count inference or global cross-owner transaction is accepted.

## 12. Failure and race walkthroughs

| Case | Required sequence and result |
|---|---|
| 1. Committed duplicate after head advances | O1 expects R5 and commits R6; another operation commits R7; equivalent O1 returns permitted R6 outcome, not stale-write Conflict or another mutation. |
| 2. Concurrent identical submissions | Both initially miss O1; authoritative uniqueness/result commit permits one mutation; the other obtains retained outcome or truthful unresolved status. |
| 3. Conflicting identity reuse | Same O1 changes target, amount, requester or original R5 expectation; owner returns Conflict without applying the changed request or leaking protected history. |
| 4. Lost owner acknowledgment | State/result/obligation commit succeeds; reply is lost; status/equivalent O1 recovers the outcome. New O2 is not automatically safe. |
| 5. Revoked result access | O1 committed while authorized; access is later revoked; retained output is withheld under current rules, while commit/effect history persists. |
| 6. Crash before actual handoff | Durable attempt exists but process dies before known acceptance; local absence of completion cannot distinguish this from case 7. Inspect/reconcile; no automatic send. |
| 7. Crash after actual handoff | Provider may act before local confirmation; recovery retains unknown and liability. Query attributed provider status or use only a separately governed proven equivalent continuation. |
| 8. Provider key expires | Local result/effect record survives but provider interval ended; retry remains blocked despite the same key. Reconcile/intervene; expiration does not create fresh intent. |
| 9. Local result/payload unavailable | Known commit envelope exists but Artifact content is unavailable; report permitted commit with retrieval limitation, never rerun to reproduce it. Missing ledger evidence is not none. |
| 10. Provider replacement | A times out; B has no shared deduplication state; preserve effect identity and uncertainty, prohibit blind redispatch to B. |
| 11. Nested retries | Parent and child attempts retry; new Executions remain linked, same intended effects retain identities, and cumulative charges/deadlines/attempt bounds do not reset. |
| 12. Approval consumed, dispatch aborts | Earlier owner consumption survives later failure; no automatic use refund. Only entitled new decisions can authorize new work. |
| 13. Effect succeeds, audit fails | Keep completed effect and pending ADR-009 custody obligation; repair the evidence operation only. Parent success depends on its required postconditions. |
| 14. Duplicate settlement/observation | Reconciler loses reply and repeats correction identity; expected revision and equivalence guard return prior result, with no second debit, release or observation application. |
| 15. Contradictory provider observations | Earlier unknown/none claim conflicts with later attributed completion; retain both and linked resolution. Investigation completion need not confirm the effect. |
| 16. Restore older storage image | Empty operation lookup after rollback lacks coverage proof; hold affected scope rather than reconstructing spend/dispatch authority from absence. |
| 17. Credential rotation or deadline expiry | Safe transport continuation still needs current Secret/authority and remaining time; otherwise stop continuation without changing prior effect to none. |
| 18. Provider reports not found | If old request may still execute or query is not authoritative, retain unknown; absence is insufficient for another potentially harmful effect. |
| 19. Compensation fails | Compensation has its own effect and uncertainty; original completion/history remains intact and both obligations remain visible. |
| 20. Partial non-Tool completion | Initialized Plugin/publication failure repairs publication; migration success/old stop failure repairs stopping under current authority. Neither repeats completed initialization or migration. |

These walkthroughs establish expected behavior, not executed test results. Cases must be instantiated against the actual storage, scheduling, provider and retention failure profile before claims are enabled.

## 13. Security, resources and residual risks

| Risk | Required treatment / accountable boundary |
|---|---|
| Identity probing across tenants | Owner verifies current tenant/access before protected status or conflict detail; IDs are not capabilities. |
| Descriptor tampering or ambiguous normalization | Owner pins interpretation and exact semantic evidence; unproven equivalence cannot authorize resend. |
| Raw Secret/private data retained for replay | Protected exact references and governed disposition; ADR-010 qualifies Secret use. No raw credential retention for historical replay. |
| Hidden SDK retries or new provider key | Adapter must prove the entire retry path; unsupported automatic retry is disabled. |
| Unbounded ledger/obligation growth | Resource owner admits storage, queries and repair with finite bounds; restrict new work before safety evidence is discarded. |
| Old authority revived by recovery | ADR-005/007 current checks and exclusive acceptance; no checkpoint grants or automatic Approval refund. |
| Provider observation overclaimed | Attributed evidence establishes only its stated target/effect extent; unknown and contradictions remain explicit. |
| Retention guarantee silently weakened | Contract/retention owners preserve commitments or expose affected gaps and block dependent retries. |

Execution/Action maintains effect lineage and safe-next-action reasoning; each state owner maintains its results; Evidence maintains custody; Resources maintains capacity/liability. No repair role gains blanket authority across them. Queries and repairs require bounded finite duties under ADR-005/006/009, not immortal system credentials.

Residual uncertainty and manual intervention are accepted operational costs within declared capabilities. Lost evidence, inadequate provider coverage or unresolved Q-07 parameters are not accepted as proof of safe replay. Review on provider changes, new failure domains, retention pressure, high reconciliation backlog or requests for distributed availability.

## 14. Solo burdens, consequences and reversibility

| Burden | A — Selected owner ledgers | B — Common journal | C — Provider extension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implementation | Owner atomic result/equivalence integration | Adds journal/owner coordination | Per-provider equivalence/status handling |
| Operations | Retention and outstanding-duty inspection | Additional journal availability/repair | Independent provider limits/outages |
| Maintenance | Versioned descriptors and historical readers | Journal and owner schema evolution | Provider contract changes |
| Testing | Concurrency, crash, retention and disclosure cases | Same cases plus journal gaps | Timeout, expiry, hidden retries and target truth |
| Local development | Reuses qualified local owner store | Additional component to run | Deterministic simulator needed before real proof |
| Deployment | Exclusive local scope and retained data | Journal topology/configuration | Credentials and qualified target scope |
| Learning curve | Distinguish operation, attempt and effect | Additional source-of-truth reasoning | Provider-specific limits cannot be generalized |

Benefits are a direct fit with accepted durability, deterministic duplicate decisions, recoverable owner outcomes and visible external uncertainty. Costs include retained metadata, exact historical interpretation, stricter admission and potentially manual reconciliation. No guarantee makes lost acknowledgment harmless without the specified evidence.

Type 1 reflects durable identity interpretation, externally performed effects, retention commitments and resource/Approval history. Replacement must quiesce/fence old dispatch authority, preserve exact scoped identities, descriptor versions, outcomes, effect lineage, coverage and obligations, validate migrated interpretation and qualify new status/dispatch behavior before resuming. Do not discard old mappings or reuse keys because a new ledger or provider is installed. Missing mappings cause explicit unavailability and intervention.

A derived cross-owner inspection journal may be added without changing authority. Moving authoritative records or widening failure scope requires a reviewed replacement with equivalent evidence. Retained readers may coexist for historical interpretation; they must not activate old code or credentials as a replay shortcut.

## 15. Implementation gates and conformance evidence

Before enabling a concrete retry/effect profile, the accountable owners must provide:

1. Versioned equivalence and identifier allocation rules, including original preconditions, normalization and protected input references.
2. Atomic owner commit/duplicate tests under concurrency and lost acknowledgment, within ADR-004's actual qualified storage scope.
3. ADR-007 dispatch participation, exclusivity, actual acceptance timing and crash-window evidence.
4. Numeric retention/validity intervals, time bases, collection policy, historical interpretation and admitted storage/resource bounds; Q-07 is not universally settled here.
5. Current disclosure and tenant isolation proof, including revoked access to known prior results.
6. Status/absence proof and visible intervention behavior for unavailable, expired, rolled-back or contradictory records.
7. Every claimed provider's qualified deduplication/status/failure coverage and full SDK/transport retry behavior; no mock alone proves a real provider.
8. Reconciliation/correction/settlement deduplication, unknown-liability conservation and ADR-009 evidence-only repair.
9. Nested-attempt, replacement-provider and compensation cases from §12, with CONF-034–037 scope and limitations recorded.

This ADR creates no runtime code, tests, schemas, API signatures, storage product choice or external-provider integration. ADR-010 supplies purpose-bound Secret mechanisms when needed. ADR-011 selects release evidence/compatibility workflow and can use these obligations without treating the decision as conformance proof. ADR-012/013 retain isolation/operational observation choices. No subsequent ADR is created here.
